# Analog Devices and Deere: company thought-leader dossier

Research cutoff: 2026-08-16. This dossier looks for public, attributable thinkers with a durable company thesis—not consensus pages, estimate aggregators, or people whose relevance is merely thematic. It intentionally scores the quality of a thinker separately from whether a particular accounting call landed.

## Result in one page

The strongest Analog Devices archive belongs to **Brian Colello**: repeated named work, an explicit analog-cycle model, revisions, a resolved directional win, and a visible numerical miss. **Mark Landecker** supplies the best owner-operator-style economic thesis, while the **Madison Investors team** provides the clearest public admission that its cycle timing was wrong before correctly identifying the bottom. **Citrini Research** is the closest independent thematic archetype, but its on-device-AI mechanism remains only partly testable from ADI's consolidated reporting.

For Deere, **Leandro of Best Anchor Stocks** is the clearest Leopold/Gill-style match: a very long initial thesis, repeated revisions, an explicit personal position, a differentiated fourth-quarter revenue inference that resolved well, and a later trim. **George Maglares** has the best recurring institutional archive. **Temple 8 Research** is highly differentiated and causally rich, but its acute March-June 2026 crisis call failed even though its segment-level PPA weakness call was right. That combination is exactly why originality and accounting accuracy should not be collapsed into one leaderboard.

### Ranking rubric

Thought-leader quality is scored out of 30: originality, causal depth, target specificity, revision discipline, archive continuity, and conflict transparency, each 0-5. Accounting accuracy is a separate evidence summary. A high score does not mean “always right.”

## Analog Devices profiles

### 1. Brian Colello — the cycle-and-moat benchmark

**Identity and edge.** Colello is Morningstar's named semiconductor analyst. His edge is not channel checks alone; it is a repeatable distinction between a temporary inventory cycle and company-specific franchise damage. His public archive ties switching costs, long product lives, automotive/industrial content, and analog design expertise to valuation.

**First dated thesis found.** On [2023-02-15](https://www.morningstar.com/company-reports/1137895-analog-devices-appears-to-be-a-share-gainer-in-analog-semis-196-fair-value-estimate), he called ADI a share gainer positioned for rising chip content in automotive, 5G, and industrial systems.

**Revisions and falsifiable record.** His [2023-08-23 note](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/analog-devices-earnings-cyclical-downturn-is-horizon-196-fair-value-estimate) correctly anticipated further demand deceleration and inventory buildup, but its roughly $3.0 billion FY2023 Q4 revenue model missed the [reported $2.716 billion](https://investor.analog.com/news-releases/news-release-details/analog-devices-reports-fiscal-fourth-quarter-and-record-fiscal). He then [reaffirmed](https://www.morningstar.com/company-reports/1194686-analog-devices-earnings-we-expect-firm-to-weather-the-current-cyclical-downturn) that ADI would weather the downturn. Revenue ultimately bottomed at [$2.16 billion in FY2024 Q2](https://www.analog.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024/5-22-2024-adi-reports-fiscal-2nd-qtr-2024-financial-results.html), and FY2025 revenue grew 17% with $4.3 billion of free cash flow in the [annual report](https://investor.analog.com/static-files/ed11172b-23b3-4639-becd-94386def2869).

His [April 2025 tariff work](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/analog-devices-stock-appears-cheap-versus-our-fair-value-less-so-if-tariffs-stay-place) is scenario analysis, not a clean point forecast: if tariffs persisted, he estimated roughly $11 billion revenue and $8 EPS in FY2026. In [February 2026](https://www.morningstar.com/company-reports/1265718-analog-devices-remains-at-the-innovation-forefront-in-analog-and-mixed-signal-semis), he raised fair value to $325 from $245 as AI demand became more material. Both remain open or conditional.

**Archive and conflicts.** The archive is unusually continuous, but some full research is subscription-gated. Morningstar sells research; cited pages carry the standard no-position disclosure. His one visible revenue miss should stay in the record rather than be averaged away.

### 2. Mark Landecker — long-duration owner economics

**Identity and edge.** Mark Landecker, CFA, is a portfolio manager at FPA Crescent. FPA's [January 2023 transcript](https://fpa.com/docs/default-source/funds/fpa-crescent-fund/quarterly-webcasts/fpa-crescent-fund-webcast-transcript-2022-12.pdf?sfvrsn=51039f9d_4) says it first bought ADI more than a decade earlier. His edge is an owner's model of the analog industry: many niche products, long lives, lower Moore's-law obsolescence, design-in switching costs, and diversified industrial/automotive demand.

**Thesis and outcome.** Landecker called ADI “something really special” and explicitly welcomed a future downcycle as an opportunity to double the position. This is falsifiable as franchise resilience, not quarterly EPS precision. FPA still reported [1.26 million shares at December 2025](https://fpa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ANNUAL-FINANCIALS-AND-OTHER-INFORMATION_FPA-Crescent-Fund-12-31-25.pdf), while ADI's post-downturn cash generation supported the durable-business thesis.

**Archive and conflicts.** Public target-specific updates are sparse and the fund has multiple managers, limiting clean author attribution. Landecker manages a fund that owns ADI; SEC materials also documented substantial personal investment in the fund. That is alignment and a conflict, not evidence of accuracy.

### 3. Madison Investors team — revision discipline

**Identity and edge.** The signed public letters identify Rich Eisinger, Haruki Toyama, and Joe Maginot. Their edge is normalized-earnings portfolio work plus unusually explicit error admission.

**Thesis history.** The [2023 year-end letter](https://madisoninvestments.com/assets/img/resources/Commentary/2023-Year-End-US-Equity-Letter.pdf) said the semiconductor downturn was worse than most forecast and called the result “mixed,” while the team kept adding on the long-term digitization and moat thesis. The [Q1 2024 letter](https://imonkey-files.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/MadisonInvestorsFund-ISL%20%281%29.pdf) said demand was still declining as inventories fell. By [Q2 2024](https://madisoninvestments.com/assets/img/investment-strategies/Quarterly/The-Madison-Quarterly-Large-Cap-Equity-SMA.pdf), it said the cycle appeared to be bottoming and expected a profit rebound. The revenue trough did occur in FY2024 Q2, followed by sequential growth and a strong FY2025.

**Archive and conflicts.** This is a team attribution, not a single celebrity analyst. Letters are continuous enough to reconstruct revisions, although some PDFs expose only a quarter rather than an exact publication day. Madison owned ADI for clients.

### 4. Citrini Research — differentiated independent thematic work

**Identity and edge.** Citrini is a stable pseudonymous research brand; a legal identity was not established and is not inferred here. Its [July 2024 memo](https://www.citriniresearch.com/p/thematic-memo-inference-on-device) explicitly went long ADI and linked Maxim's power expertise to on-device inference and a prospective iPhone replacement cycle. That is differentiated and causal rather than a copied consensus number.

**Outcome.** ADI recovered strongly, but its public consolidated accounts do not prove that iPhone replacement demand—as opposed to industrial recovery and data-center power—caused the result. The record is therefore mixed, not a clean win.

**Archive and conflicts.** Dated thematic memos and social posts exist, but parts of the archive are paid. Citrini disclosed an active long and can trade. The pseudonymous identity and mechanism-attribution problem cap the score.

### 5. Jay Goldberg — a useful public loss

**Identity and edge.** Goldberg is a semiconductor analyst at Seaport Research Partners and founder of Digits to Dollars; his [public bio](https://www.twst.com/bio/goldberg-jay/) documents industry, China, and finance experience.

**Call and revision.** A public ratings archive records his [2025-04-30 Sell initiation and $155 target](https://www.benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/analyst/680a5080914ae80001adf768/jay-goldberg). On [2025-07-21](https://www.streetinsider.com/Analyst%2BComments/Seaport%2BGlobal%2BSecurities%2BUpgrades%2BAnalog%2BDevices%2B%28ADI%29%2Bto%2BNeutral/25072601.html), he moved to Neutral and wrote that he had expected no analog-inventory improvement and a slowing economy: “We were wrong.” ADI had closed at $241.85. This is a documented loss and an unusually valuable postmortem.

**Archive and conflicts.** Full sell-side notes are largely inaccessible, so the causal history is less complete than Colello's. He is employed by a broker, and the public snippets do not preserve the full note-level conflict language.

### 6-7. Ryan Mahon and Aristotle Atlantic — promising, not yet deep enough

Ryan Mahon's [May 2024 fund letter](https://tamim.com.au/wpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/TAMIM_GlobalMobility_May_2024.pdf) identified the FY2024 Q2 inventory bottom and tied ADI to EVs, charging, and automation. The bottom call resolved well, but only one detailed public ADI thesis was found. His [fund biography](https://tamim.com.au/wpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/tamim_fund_global_mobility_-_summary_May-2024.pdf) establishes his role and background.

The Aristotle Atlantic team's [Q2 2024 thesis excerpt](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/what-makes-analog-devices-adi-a-good-investment-1325324/) connects ADI to ADAS, automation, Intelligent Edge, IoT, and sustainable energy. It is broad, secondary-hosted, and lacks a public revision chain, so it belongs on the watchlist rather than the core leaderboard.

## Deere profiles

### 1. Leandro, Best Anchor Stocks — closest archetype match

**Identity and edge.** The public identity is Leandro of Best Anchor Stocks; no legal surname was established, so none is supplied. His edge is a long-form owner archive that combines an installed-base/dealer/data moat, recurring precision revenue, financial-arm accounting, cyclically normalized cash flow, and actual position decisions.

**First thesis.** The [2024-05-14 deep dive](https://www.bestanchorstocks.com/p/deere-cultivating-technology) is roughly 120 pages and discloses Deere as the latest portfolio addition. He judged permanent-loss risk low, saw reasonable odds of double-digit long-run returns without needing technology optionality, and forecast 7%-8% free-cash-flow CAGR over five to ten years. A [June 2024 podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/sg/podcast/deere-a-wide-moat-stock-hiding-in-plain-sight-with/id1437766060?i=1000657902519) provides an additional dated public discussion.

**Revisions and outcomes.** In [January 2025](https://www.bestanchorstocks.com/p/a-fertile-ground-for-the-individual), he framed 2023 as peak, 2024 as the first down year, and 2025 as likely another decline, while arguing countercyclical cash flow and buybacks could improve the business by the next peak. FY2025 net income did fall to $5.027 billion.

His best clean call came on [2025-08-15](https://www.bestanchorstocks.com/p/the-reason-behind-the-drop): he inferred about $10.09 billion of FY2025 Q4 equipment revenue, +9% year over year, while the cited consensus expected a decline. Deere [reported $10.579 billion](https://www.deere.com/assets/pdfs/common/news/deere-4q25-earnings-release.pdf), +14%, putting his point estimate within 5% and direction clearly right. In [May 2026](https://www.bestanchorstocks.com/p/bouncing-along), he disclosed trimming an overweight position at $647 as risk-reward deteriorated. That trim demonstrates revision discipline but is not an accounting hit.

**Archive and conflicts.** This is the most complete independent Deere archive found, with the publication itself describing eight or more updates. He owns the stock, sells paid research, and uses affiliate relationships. Later performance claims are self-reported marketing, not audited evidence.

### 2. George Maglares — recurring institutional framework

**Identity and edge.** Maglares is Morningstar's named industrials analyst; his [biography](https://www.morningstar.com/people/george-maglares) documents prior analyst and portfolio-management experience. His work links the dealer moat, channel inventories, through-cycle margins, and precision-technology penetration.

**Thesis and revisions.** In early 2025 he [raised fair value from $355 to $501](https://global.morningstar.com/en-gb/stocks/10-stocks-with-largest-fair-value-estimate-increases-during-q4-earnings), called 2025 the likely earnings trough, and modeled roughly 20% through-cycle margins. In [August 2025](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/deere-earnings-north-american-ag-still-weak-company-holding-steady-prepped-2026), he kept a $550 value and argued channel stabilization boded well for 2026. Deere's [FY2026 Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/315189/000110465926064747/de-20260521xex99d1.htm) showed consolidated revenue up 5%, but large agriculture remained weak. A later note raised fair value to $600 and expected around 10% revenue CAGR as technology penetration expanded toward 2030; that remains open.

**Archive and conflicts.** The archive is recurring and named, though some reports are paid. Morningstar sells research and publishes standard ownership disclosures. The 2025-trough assertion should remain mixed until full FY2026 establishes whether earnings actually bottomed.

### 3. Temple 8 Research — excellent causal model, failed acute catalyst

**Identity and edge.** Temple 8 is a stable pseudonymous Substack research brand; legal authors were not established. Its [2026-03-17 short thesis](https://temple8capital.substack.com/p/john-deere-de-short-thesis-shadow-bank) is the most differentiated Deere bear case found. It treats Deere as both manufacturer and “shadow bank,” connecting sell-in, 2023 lease vintages, used-equipment residuals, dealer balance sheets, JDCC reserves, and buyback capacity.

**Split verdict.** The segment call was good: Temple 8 said the headline masked PPA weakness, and [FY2026 Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/315189/000110465926064747/de-20260521xex99d1.htm) delivered PPA sales down 14% and operating profit down 39% even as consolidated revenue rose 5%.

The acute crisis call was not good. It said the dealer seizure began in March, set a March-June catalyst window, and disclosed June puts. Deere maintained guidance. JDCC's [quarterly filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27673/000110465926067314/jdcc-20260503x10q.htm) showed the annualized provision ratio improving to 0.65% from 0.70%, wholesale receivables declining as dealer inventory fell, and 30-day delinquencies nearly unchanged. Deere closed [2026-07-17 near $597](https://www.investing.com/equities/deere---co-historical-data), versus roughly $593 at publication. The acute call is contradicted through its own window, even though part of its operating diagnosis was right.

**Archive and conflicts.** One exceptional deep dive is not a multi-year company history. The author is pseudonymous, sells research, explicitly bought puts, and may trade without notice.

### 4. Kristen Owen — sector specialist with a clean macro-cycle call

**Identity and edge.** Kristen Owen, CFA, is Oppenheimer's named Deere analyst on [Deere's coverage list](https://investor.deere.com/stock/default.aspx). Her [LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kristenowencfa) and repeated earnings-call appearances establish persistent sector coverage. Her questions focus on price realization, used inventory, production, Brazil, and decremental margins.

**Resolved call.** In a [2024-02-15 interview](https://finance.yahoo.com/video/why-john-deere-bellwether-despite-152128085.html), she forecast FY2024 equipment sales down closer to 20%. Deere [reported](https://www.deere.com/en/news/all-news/fy24-fourth-quarter-earnings/) $44.759 billion versus $55.565 billion, a 19.45% decline. That is the cleanest named sell-side Deere accounting call in this search.

**Archive and conflicts.** Ratings and management questions are public, but full notes are not, limiting reconstruction of revisions. She is a sell-side analyst at Oppenheimer.

### 5. Shawn O'Malley — valuable because he passed

O'Malley's [2025-10-06 Intrinsic Value analysis](https://www.theinvestorspodcast.com/intrinsic-value/john-deere-de/) explicitly normalizes a cyclical manufacturer rather than extrapolating peak earnings. It maps the brand and dealer moat, precision recurring revenue, and financial-services leverage, then concludes Deere is approximately fairly valued and too uncertain to add. He estimated that roughly 5.5% free-cash-flow growth plus yield could support about a 10% return, but did not force a purchase. The causal discipline is good; the Deere archive is only one issue and the long-horizon economics remain open.

### 6-7. Annabel Riggs and Steven Fisher — watchlist

Firetrail portfolio manager Annabel Riggs presented Deere as a high-quality cyclical and discussed bull, bear, and what the market missed in a [February 2025 podcast](https://www.livewiremarkets.com/wires/all-ears-on-deere-the-investment-case-for-john-deere). Her [fund material](https://firetrail.com/wp-content/uploads/Firetrail-S3-Global-Opportunities-Fund-Flyer.pdf) establishes identity and the fund's Deere exposure. The public summary is too compressed for a clean falsifiable extraction, so she is ranked but not placed in the JSONL hit-rate sample.

UBS analyst Steven Fisher expected initial FY2026 net-income guidance of $4.4-$5.1 billion in a [2025-11-25 note summary](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/ubs-reiterates-buy-rating-on-deere-stock-with-535-price-target-93CH-4377734). Deere instead issued [$4.0-$4.75 billion](https://www.deere.com/assets/pdfs/common/news/deere-4q25-earnings-release.pdf). It is a useful resolved loss, but public evidence is mostly ratings summaries and does not establish a deep Deere thought-leadership archive.

## Method and backtest cautions

The companion JSONL stores 28 dated or period-dated claim events from 13 source identities: 15 ADI events from seven identities and 13 Deere events from six. The rankings assess one additional Deere profile, Annabel Riggs, whose public excerpt was too compressed to add to the claim ledger. It includes initial theses, revisions, and postmortems because a thought leader is a time series, not a single quote. Exact dates are omitted when a primary PDF reliably exposes only a reporting period. Evidence quotes are capped at 25 words.

Do not turn every row into a hit-rate denominator. `not_testable` process disclosures and `open` long-horizon theses should be excluded. Conditional risk scenarios require the stated condition to persist. A segment call and a consolidated crisis call from the same article can resolve differently. Market-price outcomes do not substitute for accounting outcomes unless the original claim was explicitly about price.

The JSONL fields are: `claim_id`, leader identity, company/ticker, platform and source, publication date or source period, retrieval date, claim stage/type/topic, claim/evidence/causal model/test/horizon, outcome and source, accounting accuracy, thought-leader score, archive completeness, conflicts, and notes. Unknown fields are omitted rather than guessed.

### Search limitations

- Search indexing and paywalls make absence claims provisional. “No qualifying source found” does not prove no post exists.
- X and LinkedIn archives are incomplete without authenticated, stable post URLs. No claim was created from a search-result snippet alone.
- Fund letters can be team-authored. The dossier uses the signatures printed on the relevant documents and labels them as team calls.
- Sell-side secondary pages establish named actions but often omit the full thesis and disclosures. Those records receive lower archive scores and explicit provenance notes.
- Public accounting can resolve revenue, segment profit, credit provisions, and guidance; it often cannot isolate market share, installed-base data, or a specific device-cycle mechanism.
