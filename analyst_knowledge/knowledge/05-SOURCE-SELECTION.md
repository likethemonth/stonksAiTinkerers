# Best available sources by target company

> Ranking cutoff: 2026-08-16. “Ranked” requires at least three eligible observations. Mean errors below are scaled absolute errors; different metrics and horizons are pooled only in the explicitly broad source-selection table.

This chapter answers **who is best for the next reported number**. It does not answer **who understands the company best**. The person-first answer is in [07-THOUGHT-LEADERS.md](07-THOUGHT-LEADERS.md); the two lists are deliberately separate.

## Home Depot

**Company thought leader:** Alex Morris (TSOH) is the closest match to a deep, revision-aware company thinker; Jaime Katz is the strongest public model-based alternative. Neither has enough matured thesis families for a definitive statistical rank.

1. **Benzinga Pro analyst consensus** is the strongest currently rankable exact-accounting cohort: 6 observations across sales and EPS, 3.88% mean scaled error, broad score 0.956. It is an aggregate benchmark, not a named analyst.
2. **Hedgeye Retail Sector** is the only independently authored/firm-level cohort with six observations, but its broad 34.60% mean error and -1.71 percentage-point mean consensus skill are materially worse. Its long-horizon sales/EPS levels were more useful than its sales-growth calls; do not apply one pooled weight blindly.
3. **Christopher Horvers (JPMorgan)** has one successful relative-comps directional call; **W. Andrew Carter** and **Value Voyager** each have one numeric observation. All are provisional.
4. For the current quarter, consensus, Home Depot guidance, and Brian Nagel’s disclosed EPS estimate are the useful point inputs. Nagel has no comparable closed history in this public corpus, so he receives no “best analyst” badge.

Verdict: use consensus as the numerical anchor; use Hedgeye as a differentiated scenario source, not as a superior point estimator.

## Analog Devices

**Company thought leader:** Brian Colello has the strongest public ADI cycle-and-moat archive found. His record includes both a correct downturn/recovery framing and a visible FY2023 Q4 revenue miss.

1. **Stifel’s research team** has two realized-revenue calls with 4.91% mean error and one successful above-threshold directional call. It remains provisional.
2. **Cantor Fitzgerald’s research team** has two forecasts of guidance issued at earnings with 2.80% mean error. These are promising but belong to the separate `guidance_issuance` class, not realized-quarter accuracy.
3. A preserved **Wall Street consensus** snapshot has two realized metrics with 0.31% mean error, also provisional. **Brian Colello** has one own-model revenue call with 10.44% error.
4. SemiAnalysis and Serenity are thematically relevant to semiconductors but produced no qualifying ADI call in the tested public corpora.

Verdict: management guidance plus point-in-time consensus is the defensible current anchor; monitor Stifel and Cantor, but the sample is too small to declare a winning individual analyst.

## Hays plc

**Company thought leader:** Graham Neary has the most differentiated open structural thesis; Paul Scott has the strongest resolved public judgment. The sparse Hays archive does not support a proven single-company oracle.

1. **Hays company-compiled sell-side consensus** is rankable across the three target-style FY2025 metrics: 3 observations, 11.34% mean error, broad score 0.917. EPS drove most of the miss; net fees were very close.
2. **Jefferies Equity Research** has one adjusted-EBIT observation with 5.70% error and +17.98 percentage-point skill versus the contemporaneous consensus. This is the most interesting independent call, but one result is not a track record.
3. **Bloomberg Hays consensus** has one close operating-profit observation; UBS has one materially high long-horizon call; Neil Shah has one successful directional deterioration thesis.

Verdict: the company-published current nine-analyst panel is the best exact-metric source. Use Jefferies as a monitored challenger, not a ranked winner.

## Deere

**Company thought leader:** Leandro of Best Anchor Stocks is the closest Gill/Aschenbrenner-style match: long-form causal work, disclosed ownership, repeated updates, a differentiated numerical win, and a later trim. His status remains provisional because only three thesis families were found.

No source clears the three-observation threshold. Benzinga Pro consensus has two observations at 4.46% mean error; an Investing.com-reported consensus has two at 7.41%; a separate article-reported Wall Street consensus has two at 9.22%. Kristen Owen’s one long-horizon equipment-sales decline estimate was close (2.83% error) but substantially echoed Deere’s own guidance. Steven Fisher’s initial-guidance range call is a different outcome class.

Verdict: no defensible “best analyst” can be named from public history. Use exact-metric consensus plus Deere segment guidance, and keep worldwide revenue distinct from equipment net sales.

## Requested personalities and hedge funds

Serenity, SemiAnalysis, and Keith Gill receive **not-ranked / no target observations**, not zero scores. Zero would imply failed forecasts; the evidence instead shows absence of eligible target-company claims. Public hedge-fund holdings are also excluded because delayed ownership disclosures are not forecasts. Hedgeye qualifies as a research-firm source through explicit dated model calls, not through a fund position.
