01 · The task

Predict a number the company has not published yet.

Twelve metrics across four companies. The ML lens trains only on the pipeline's own extracted observation table — reported actuals and disclosed guidance — never on prose.

What makes it hard

A decade of quarterly history is 40 observations, and excluding the COVID whipsaw removes eight. This is a small-sample problem wearing a big-data costume, so every technique that assumes abundant data is a trap.

The rule we set first

A model may only speak if it beats a naive baseline and clears a gate fixed before it ran. Anything else abstains. A lens that knows when to shut up is worth more than one that always emits a number.

02 · Attempt one

Year-over-year growth. It lost to doing nothing.

The obvious framing: predict y_t / y_{t−4} − 1 from lagged growth and quarter dummies.

MetricHoldout MAPESeasonal naiveVerdict
HD net sales5.40%5.24%worse than naive
HD GAAP EPS5.20%4.76%worse than naive

Losing to the baseline is information, not embarrassment. It says the target was wrong, not the estimator. Two causes, both visible in the data: COVID puts +23% to +35% growth rows in training, and the SRS and GMS acquisitions add a step to year-over-year that appears for four quarters then vanishes — straddling the holdout exactly.

03 · Attempt two

Sequential ratios, and validating the actual task.

  • Reframe the target to y_t / y_{t−1}. Seasonality makes it tight, and an acquisition sits in both quarters once annualised, so the ratio is acquisition-neutral.
  • Exclude COVID target rows as a documented exogenous shock, not silent winsorising.
  • Match validation to deployment. We were averaging four different transitions; the task is one — Q1 to Q2. Grading on Q4-to-Q1 inflates the error for a problem the model never has to solve.

04 · The model zoo

Five estimators, three ensembles, chosen to fail differently.

Ridge (α=1.0), Random Forest (300 trees, depth 3), Gradient Boosting (200, depth 2, lr 0.05), SVR with an RBF kernel (C=10) and distance-weighted KNN. Kernel and instance-based learners fail differently from linear and tree learners, which is the precondition for ensembling to help. 05 · The uncomfortable result

Ensembling bought almost nothing.

1.64%Best single modelRandom forest
1.43%Best ensembleCV-weighted voting
6.04%StackingWorst of everything

Voting moved the needle by two tenths of a point. Stacking — the most sophisticated member — was the worst performer by a distance, because a ridge meta-learner fitted on roughly twenty rows overfits exactly as small-sample theory predicts.

Estimator choice was never the bottleneck. That finding is what redirected the work, and it is the single most useful thing the model zoo produced.

06 · The reframe

Ask what is genuinely unknown.

Home Depot reports on 18 August. The US Census building-materials category level for HD's exact fiscal quarter is published on 14 August. So the category is already known — the only unknown is Home Depot's share of it. Strip out SRS and GMS wholesale revenue, which sits outside that category, and organic share is remarkably stable quarter to quarter.

share_t = share_{t−4} × (share_{t−1} ÷ share_{t−5}) sales_t = share_t × category_t + inorganic_t FY2026Q2: 0.3197 × 0.9732 = 0.3111 0.3111 × 141,088 + 3,880 = $47,775M

Zero fitted parameters. Nothing is estimated, so nothing can be overfit. Q2 walk-forward across six ex-COVID years: 0.38, 0.46, 0.61, 0.18, 0.63, 0.11 percent.

07 · The same move, three times

Every model that passed exploits something already published.

Home Depot — share of category

Census NAICS 444 for the quarter publishes four days before HD reports. Model the share, not the level. 0.40% over six periods.

Analog Devices — guidance realisation

ADI guides a quarter ahead, so only the realisation ratio is unknown. Multiply the guide by the expanding mean of past ratios. 1.38% over fourteen.

Hays — quarterly composition

Three of four quarters are already disclosed in trading updates. Compose the year from its own growth rates. 1.52% over four.

Deere — nothing published

Guides full-year net income, never quarterly segments. Only history is available, and history cannot answer. Abstains.

08 · Validation protocol

The model never sees its own future.

Chronological only — a random split on a time series lets a model read the answer. At every step the model is refit on data strictly before the period it predicts.

10 · The abstention

Proving it is the data, not the model.

Deere lost to its naive baseline. Normally that means iterate. So we measured the intrinsic floor: the dispersion of the target transition's own ratio history. If the Q2→Q3 ratio has coefficient of variation c, no estimator using only that history can average better than roughly c.

Deere metricIntrinsic floorGateModel achieved
Revenue8.1%2.0%7.263%
Diluted EPS16.7%5.0%24.254%
PPA operating profit37.2%10.0%24.185%

Every gate is unreachable by construction. With four times the samples after a data fix, revenue scored 7.26% against a predicted floor of 8.1% — landing exactly on the floor. That is an information limit, not a tuning failure, and it is why Deere is carried by the driver and anchor lenses instead.

11 · Honesty

Where this is weakest.

  • Specification hindsight. The per-fold refit is clean, but we chose the framing, the model family and the COVID exclusion knowing how the whole period turned out. A forecaster in 2021 could not have known to exclude 2021.
  • Variant sensitivity. Four plausible share variants span 0.39% to 1.23% on Q2. The fair out-of-sample expectation is that range, not the headline. On this specific quarter they disagree by 2.5%.
  • Data vintage. The backtest reads revised Census values; deployment reads the advance estimate. That makes 0.40% slightly optimistic.
  • Small n. Six periods on the best model, four on Hays. Wide error bars on the error bars.
  • A bug the gates missed. Hays tags quarterly updates with the fiscal year, so keying on period collapsed a twenty-point series into six and we wrongly called Hays unmodelable. Found because it was challenged, not because a test caught it.

12 · The lesson

Information beat estimators, every time.

HD net sales category published 4 days early → 0.40% pass ADI revenue guidance published a quarter early → 1.38% pass HAS net fees 3 of 4 quarters disclosed → 1.52% pass DE revenue nothing published → 8.1% floor fail

Model family, feature engineering and ensembling moved results by tenths of a point. Access to something already published moved them by an order of magnitude. The ML work earned its place by establishing where the ceiling was without the reframe — which is what made the reframe measurable rather than merely asserted.
